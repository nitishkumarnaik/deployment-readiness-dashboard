# Health & fitness app market: what wins in 2026

Research date: November 2026. Most revenue figures for private apps are third-party estimates (Sensor Tower, AppMagic, Business of Apps and similar). They disagree with each other, so treat them as directional. Sources are listed at the end.

## 1. The market in five numbers

| Signal | Figure | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Health & fitness in-app spending, 2025 | **$4.5B, +13% YoY** (Sensor Tower) | People are paying more… |
| Health & fitness installs, 2025 | **3.96B, +0.8% YoY** (Sensor Tower) | …but downloads are flat. Growth comes from monetising intent, not chasing installs. |
| Day-30 retention, category median | **~3–6%** (strong apps 8–12%) | Retention is the hardest problem in the category. |
| Monthly subscription churn, fitness | **8.2% (2023) → 11.7% (2025)** (Sensor Tower) | Users are getting pickier. Generic apps bleed. |
| Trial → paid conversion, Health & Fitness | **~38% median, 68% choose annual** (RevenueCat via secondary source) | Once users see value in a trial, they pay, and they pay yearly. |

## 2. Who is winning, and why

| App | Scale (estimates) | The success pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Strava** | ~$415–500M ARR, confidential IPO filing in 2026 | Identity + community. Your activity is social currency. Bought Runna to add coached plans. |
| **MyFitnessPal** | ~$159M store revenue in 2025, #1 grossing | Database moat. Bought **Cal AI** in 2026 to fix its weak spot: logging friction. |
| **Cal AI** | ~$30M+ revenue in year one, acquired | Took the #1 pain point (manual food logging) and removed it with photo AI, then grew it with creator and influencer marketing. |
| **Flo** | ~$123M; #1 health app by downloads in 2025 | Women's health is recurring and life-stage specific, so retention is built in. |
| **Oura** | $908M FY2025 revenue (S-1), 5M+ paid members | Hardware + subscription; sleep and recovery data. |
| **Finch** | ~$0.4M → ~$1M per month (Jun 2025 → Jun 2026), bootstrapped | Emotional attachment (a pet you care for) turns self-care into a game. Free core, optional Plus subscription. |
| **Shotsy** (GLP-1 tracker) | 1M+ downloads, 4.8★, $9.99/mo or $39.99/yr | A hot niche, beautiful shareable charts, $0 ad spend, word of mouth. |
| **Hevy** | Grown organically to a reported ~$0.2–0.8M per month | Free core logger + community; no paid ads. |
| **Ladder** | Among the top grossing fitness apps in 2026 | Coach-led strength programming in a team format. |
| **Calm** | ~$210M, **−24% YoY** | Warning sign: generic meditation is saturated. |

### Patterns that repeat

1. **Remove a painful step.** Cal AI (photo logging) and Shotsy (one-tap dose logging) won on friction, not features.
2. **Own a niche with recurring need.** GLP-1, women's health and strength training all have a weekly or monthly reason to come back.
3. **Make progress shareable.** Strava activities and Shotsy charts are the marketing.
4. **Emotional hooks beat streak guilt.** Finch's pet and Strava's kudos keep people coming back without shaming them.
5. **Free core, paid depth, annual plans.** Most winners give the daily loop away and charge for plans, insights and history.
6. **Generic is dying.** Calm is shrinking while specific, outcome-led apps grow.

## 3. Why users quit (the gap to design against)

- **Manual logging burden** and long sign-ups: the most-cited cause of abandonment. About 70% of users stop within ~100 days, with the steepest drop in the first two weeks.
- **Shame and unreachable goals**: users feel judged logging "bad" foods, or give up on app-set targets they can't hit (British Journal of Health Psychology study, Oct 2025).
- **No accountability** and **no visible results within a few weeks**.
- **Monotony** (about 16% cite repetitive routines) and battery drain.
- **Quitting after reaching a goal**: the app had no role beyond the goal.

## 4. Where demand is heading

| Trend | Signal |
|---|---|
| **GLP-1 medication** | The strongest signal in 2026. About 13% of US adults have used a GLP-1 drug; trend trackers show GLP-1 interest up ~159%; companion apps are a fast-emerging category. Noom moved into GLP-1 support. Tablet forms (e.g. oral semaglutide) widen the audience further. |
| **AI coaching on top of wearables** | Google's Gemini health coach, Oura, Whoop. Big players are moving in, so a small team should not compete head-on. |
| **Strength training** | Mainstream; Ladder and Hevy are growing. |
| **Sleep & recovery** | Oura's growth; sleep moving into regular programming. |
| **Women's health, peri/menopause** | Fast-growing, high retention. |
| **AI nutrition** | Among the top apps by IAP revenue growth in 2025. |

## 5. The opening we chose

GLP-1 medications can cause meaningful loss of lean mass along with fat. Clinicians commonly advise **adequate protein and resistance training** to protect muscle. Yet the GLP-1 apps on the market are mostly **injection logs and medication-level charts**, and fitness apps ignore the dose cycle.

Nobody connects the two: *"Your nausea peaks the day after your dose, so your strength sessions move to Thursday and Saturday, and on low-appetite days here's how to still hit your protein floor."*
